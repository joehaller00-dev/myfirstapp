## Why this matters
1. **Compare-at prices that were never charged are fake reference prices.** The FTC's Guides Against Deceptive Pricing (16 CFR Part 233) say a former price must be one the item was openly and actively offered at, in good faith, for a reasonably substantial period. Several states (e.g. California Bus. & Prof. Code §17501) restrict "was" prices to prices charged in the prior 90 days. Showing "$146.99 → $123.99" on an item that sold for $36.99 in August is the textbook case. Remove compare-at prices store-wide unless the item genuinely sold at that price recently. (Not legal advice; a lawyer confirms.)
2. **A 12-17x markup on a commodity item is visible to buyers.** The same items are a Google Lens search away (see competitors.md). That is the "trust" objection 88 of 100 panel buyers raised.
