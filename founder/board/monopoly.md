# Monopoly lens
Score: 2/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- **The gross margin on paper is wide.** Recorded unit costs run 5x to 17x below retail. That gap is the only real asset here, but under this lens wide margins on generic goods are not a moat. They are a signal to copycats, and competition usually closes them.
- **There is one channel that works.** Google organic is the only source that converts (about 0.37%, 7 of 14 orders), and it rests on 31 blog posts and a large indexed catalogue. Content-led search is the only durable distribution asset in sight. It could compound.
- **Average order value is climbing** ($56, then $533, then $995) and a $1,170 four-pendant order shows that buyers of statement lighting exist. On 14 orders this is a hint, not a trend.
- **No inventory risk.** Dropshipping keeps the downside small while the founder looks for a niche.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **There is no monopoly anywhere: the business is a commodity reseller in an open market.** The store sells 1,819 supplier-fulfilled SKUs across about 100 product types, from chandeliers to Halloween inflatables and shower curtains, all shipped by third parties. A ceiling light that costs $8.66 and sells for $123.99 is almost certainly a generic factory item that anyone with a Shopify account can list. Whether these exact products are sold elsewhere, and at what prices, is open; /founder-competitors answers it. If they are, buyers can find the same item cheaper with one image search. None of the four sources of durability applies. There is no proprietary product, no network effect, no scale advantage (Shopify Basic, 28 customers) and no brand yet. Nothing stops a copycat in month six, because the copycat may already exist.
2. **Distribution is not solved, and the competition for it is big and well funded.** About 14,000 sessions produced 14 real orders. Strip out bots and that is still roughly 0.1%. Paid acquisition has never been tested, so CAC is unknown (/founder-cfo, after a small paid test). Organic search for "pendant light" or "chandelier" is contested by large home-goods retailers and marketplaces with far more authority and faster delivery. A store with 10 to 26 business days of delivery and buyer-paid returns is competing on price and selection against the players who own price and selection.
3. **No secret, and no small market to own first.** The brief states no insight that others lack. The seven trade pages (designers, contractors, hospitality, short-term rentals, offices, retail) spread a solo founder across seven markets at once, with no trade revenue in any of them. A 29% refund rate by count (4 of 14) also hints at a quality or expectation gap with unknown suppliers. That is the opposite of building a brand.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Pick five bestsellers. Can a buyer find the identical product elsewhere, and at what price? (/founder-competitors)
- What do you know about lighting buyers that the big retailers do not? If the answer is nothing yet, which narrow segment could you learn it in?
- What is the one small market you could hold 30%+ of within 18 months? Candidates: short-term-rental lighting packages, a single style niche, or the Oklahoma City designer trade.
- How do the first 100 customers arrive, and what does each one cost? (/founder-cfo for CAC and payback, /founder-consumer for who actually buys)
- Why did 4 of 14 orders refund? What would a stricter supplier QC process cost?
- Why now? What has changed in lighting retail that opens a gap for a new store in 2026?
- What would make a Nora product impossible to buy anywhere else: exclusive designs, a custom or made-to-order line, or curated bundles with design service?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable: the vote is PASS. The founder can bring it back with three things:
1. **One niche, named and narrow**, with the catalogue cut to the few hundred SKUs that serve it. For example, statement pendants for short-term-rental owners, sold as room packages.
2. **A differentiator a copycat cannot list overnight**: exclusive or private-label designs, custom work (the Custom Lighting Studio page already exists), or a design service bundled with the product.
3. **Proof of repeatable distribution**: 30+ real, non-refunded orders from a known channel, with a CAC measured against margin after shipping, fees and returns (/founder-cfo).
