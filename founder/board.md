# Board verdict: Nora Furnish

**Vote: 3 PASS (Offers 3, Monopoly 2, Product 3). Average score 2.7 / 10.**

This is a verdict on the store as it runs today, not on the founder or on lighting as a category. All three members said what would bring it back to the table, and those conditions are below.

## Risks more than one member raised (most dangerous first)
1. **No reason to trust it at these prices (all three).** An unknown brand, no visible proof, $300-$1,000+ fixtures from suppliers nobody has named or inspected. Data: about 0.1% of sessions convert, and 4 of 14 real orders were refunded (29%, a tiny sample).
2. **It is a commodity catalog that competes on discounts (all three).** 1,819 SKUs across about 100 product types (chandeliers next to Halloween inflatables and shower curtains), 8 live discount mechanisms and compare-at anchors. If the same items are a Google Lens search away at a fraction of the price, the margin disappears. Open question for /founder-competitors.
3. **The business owns none of the experience after checkout (Offers, Product).** 10-12 business days for "in stock", about 6 weeks for made-to-order, packaging and condition left to the supplier, and buyer-paid returns on bulky, fragile fixtures.
4. **No focus (all three).** Seven trade verticals, six custom service lines, three customer types, zero trade revenue. The homepage drew 3,275 sessions and 0 orders.
5. **Distribution is unproven (Offers, Monopoly).** Google organic is the only channel that converts (0.37%). CAC is unknown because no real paid test has run.

## Where the board disagrees
- **Which buyer.** The Offers lens sees the real pain in trade buyers (designers, STR hosts, contractors) on deadlines. The Product lens says start with the experience that already works: a homeowner searching for a specific statement pendant or wall light. The Monopoly lens wants a niche small enough to own: STR room packages, one style, or the Oklahoma City designer trade.
- **What to do with the margin.** Offers: spend it on a guarantee, speed and free returns. Monopoly: it is not a moat, and competitors will compete it away unless the product is exclusive. Product: spend it on quality, or lower prices to match what the product really is.

## Conditions checklist (the rest of the pack tests these)
- [ ] Find out whether the top 5 products are sold elsewhere and at what price (/founder-competitors)
- [ ] Pick ONE target buyer and ONE core category (the data points to statement pendants and wall lights) (/founder-consumer, /founder-marketing)
- [ ] Cut the catalog to roughly 50-300 SKUs that serve that buyer, each one the founder would hang at home; remove off-niche lines (/founder-ops)
- [ ] Get a landed cost per order: supplier cost, supplier shipping, processing fees, discounts and returns (/founder-cfo)
- [ ] Price so quality matches price, either by raising quality (sampled, inspected SKUs) or lowering price (/founder-pricing)
- [ ] Replace the discount stack with one clear price plus value the buyer can see (/founder-offer)
- [ ] An affordable guarantee: ship-by date, free returns on lighting, pre-ship inspection (/founder-offer, /founder-cfo)
- [ ] Honest delivery promises instead of "in stock" labels on items with zero inventory (/founder-ops)
- [ ] Visible proof: reviews, installed-fixture photos, named projects (/founder-launch, /founder-marketing)
- [ ] A differentiator a copycat cannot list overnight: exclusive/private-label designs, custom work, or bundled design help (/founder-offer, /founder-brand)
- [ ] Measure refund reasons on every order; target under 10% across 30+ real orders in the focus category (/founder-ops, /founder-launch)
- [ ] A measured CAC from a small, controlled paid test, compared with contribution per order (/founder-cfo, /founder-launch)

## The strongest version the board can see
Not the store that exists today. The strongest version is a **focused statement-lighting brand**: about 100 pendants, chandeliers and wall lights, each sampled in hand and photographed honestly, with real specs (dimensions, lumens, UL/ETL, bulb, install) and real delivery dates. It sells at one clear price, with free returns on lighting and a written ship-by promise paid for out of the margin. It sells to two buyers who want the same thing: design-minded homeowners finishing a room, and a small trade/short-term-rental program offering room bundles, sourcing help and trade pricing. That turns the existing trade pages from decoration into a channel. Over time, the best sellers move into a stocked or private-label line so they can't be found under another name on AliExpress. That is a different business from the 1,819-SKU general store, and the board is saying so plainly: polishing the current catalog will not fix the numbers.
