## What would flip a no

- Proof that it's a different product from the Amazon lookalikes (not the same factory item rebranded), plus a cheap sample or swatch I can hold, or a trade price that gets it close to Amazon pricing.
- A trade-account price clearly below Wayfair or Amazon for the same style, plus a UL listing number I can check myself and proof that returns on a damaged fixture are handled without a fight.
- Proof that it is clearly better made than the Amazon lookalikes (side-by-side photos, materials, how the paper and LED hold up after a year), or a price nearer $180.
- The dining room being painted and ready, plus a review photo of this exact pendant over a table in a room like mine. Or a cheaper sample of the paper material.
- A trade price under about $120 for this style, or proof that it's a different build from the lookalikes (not just 'inspected'), plus a bulk price for 4 or more units across my two rentals.
- Seeing the same pendant on Wayfair, Amazon or Lumens with a few hundred reviews, or a designer I follow showing it installed in a real home.
- Photos of this exact pendant over a kitchen island from a real buyer, plus a sizing recommendation for my kitchen. If the shade were a more durable material and the price were under about $200, I'd order it before the electrician comes.
- Seeing the same pendant on Wayfair or Amazon with a few hundred reviews, or the store sending me a cheap physical sample of the shade to hold up in my room first.
- Proof this isn't the generic factory piece, such as a cheap sample of the paper and diffuser, or a side-by-side with the Amazon version showing a real difference in materials and light quality.
- A side-by-side showing what makes this one different from the cheap lookalikes (materials, LED quality, the sampling and inspection), or a price closer to $150, or a bigger discount for buying several fixtures for the house.
- A first-order promo code or sale bringing it to about $190 or less, plus me verifying the UL number on UL's own site.
